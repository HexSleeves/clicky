## Problem Statement

Adult children spend hours every month doing tech support over the phone for aging parents who "don't even google." When the kid isn't available, parents either give up or pay GeekSquad ~$50/visit, ~$100-150/yr/household. The current Clicky codebase — a Mac menu-bar AI companion with voice + screenshot + cursor pointing — is reframed from a generalist Farza-fork into the foundation for a two-sided remote co-pilot built specifically for the bottom-quartile-tech-user (non-googling 65+ Mac users) and their adult children.

## Demand Evidence

- **Founder-as-customer:** Founder confirmed yes to "$20/mo for 80% reduction in Mom-and-Dad 'general queries' calls" — passes the only demand test that matters at pre-product.
- **Status-quo paid competitor:** Parents already pay GeekSquad ~$50/visit, "couple of times a year" → $100-150/yr revealed willingness-to-pay for "patient stranger who can find things."
- **Behavioral pain (verbatim, do not paraphrase):** Founder's parents (~65) "are terrible at finding things when I am describing it over the phone" and "they don't even google."
- **Sunday May 10, 2026:** User-research session scheduled with founder's parents. Doubles as v0 install opportunity and first observation hour.

## Status Quo

1. **Phone call to the kid** — frequent, free to parents, costs the kid time/focus/DND.
2. **GeekSquad** — ~$100-150/yr/household, slow, makes parent feel old, intermittent.
3. **Give up** — most common, hardest to measure, biggest hidden cost in lost capability.

Apple Voice Control and macOS native accessibility tools assume the user can navigate menus to enable them — which non-googling seniors cannot do.

## Target User & Wedge

**Wedge user (buyer + active user):** Adult child of a 65+ parent who fields "general queries" support calls more than once a week. Founder is patient zero. First test cohort: founder's parents (Sunday May 10 install) + 5-10 friends with parents in same age range.

**Wedge product:** Real-time remote co-pilot for the support call itself. Mom presses one button → kid sees her screen + hears her voice → **kid's clicks on a streamed preview of Mom's screen relay over the network and the existing Clicky cursor overlay flies to those coordinates on Mom's actual screen, accompanied by kid's voice**. No remote CGEvent injection in Phase 1 — cursor is visual-only, exactly as Clicky already does it locally today. Mom still does the actual clicking, kid is pointing.

**v1 task scope (the three tasks Mom and Dad call about most):** TBD from Sunday observation. Constraint: pick the top 3 from Sunday and hand-tune those before generalizing.

## Hard Constraints

- **Senior-side UX:** voice-first, one-button trigger, no typing, no menus, no settings panel visible. If a feature requires Mom to read a menu, it doesn't ship.
- **Two-sided product:** parent-side and kid-side are distinct app targets in the same Xcode project. Kid is the buyer and the actively engaged user. Every UX decision flows from this.
- **Distribution is in-person:** kid sets up Mom's Mac during a visit. No app-store funnel for the senior. Self-serve onboarding for the kid only.
- **PII / financial / health redaction is a Phase 1 requirement, not a future polish:** before any frame leaves Mom's Mac, a client-side filter blocks streaming when the active app or URL matches a blocked list — banking apps, health portals, password managers, Apple Pay, browser windows on `*.bank`, `*.health`, etc. Default-blocked, kid-side dashboard shows "stream paused — financial app detected." If Mom is on her bank, she can still call kid normally; the screen just doesn't share. Non-negotiable.
- **TCC permissions are a kid-installs-in-person checklist** (see Distribution Plan) — Mom cannot grant Screen Recording / Accessibility / Microphone alone.
- **Audio routing default:** Mom's built-in Mac mic + built-in speakers. Do not attempt AirPods pairing in v1. If she has a headset she already uses, kid configures it during install.
- **No voice-only confirmation in financial / health context.** Phase 3+ AI actions inside any app or URL on the redaction-blocklist require an explicit panel-button tap, never a voice "yes." Closes the social-engineering surface where a phone caller could trick Mom into voice-confirming a transaction.
- **Phase 1 latency budget:** kid-click → Mom-cursor lands < 250ms p95 on US residential broadband. Above that, the cursor feels disconnected from the voice. If WebRTC P2P can't hit it on representative networks, fall back to TURN-relayed or downscale Phase 1 to "annotated screenshots" instead of live cursor.
- **Confirmation UX is non-negotiable for any Phase 3+ AI action.** Phase 1 has no AI driving actions, so this constraint binds at the moment AI assist ships, not before.
- **Trust is fragile:** one wrong AI click on a payment screen erodes parent trust permanently. The redaction policy + Phase 3 confirmation UX exist to prevent this.
- **WCAG 2.1 AA target on senior-side surface:** voice-first means contrast/text-size on what little visual UI exists must clear AA. Kid-side surface is a normal Mac app, no special bar.
- **Reuse the existing Clicky pipeline:** cursor overlay, voice/audio, screenshot, TTS, Worker proxy. Do not rebuild what works.
- **Codebase is a starting point, not the product.** Diverges from Farza's clicky after this branch.

## Premises

P1. Founder-as-customer ($20/mo yes) is sufficient demand evidence to start; Sunday validates user side.
P2. Buyer is the adult child, not the senior. Two-sided product.
P3. Senior-side UX is voice-first, one-button, no-typing, no-settings — hard rule.
P4. Phase 1 is kid-driven, AI-free, visual-pointing-only. Ships in 2-3 weeks.
P5. Distribution is in-person installation by the kid. No app-store funnel for the parent.
P6. Codebase is a starting point. Product diverges from Farza's clicky.
P7. AI is the upgrade ladder across Phases 2-4 — visual suggestions, then confirmed actions, then autonomous routine tasks. Each phase only ships if the prior phase shows retention.

## Recommended Build — Phased

Single linear scope, one naming convention. Each phase is its own go/no-go gate.

### Phase 1 — "Walkie-Talkie with Magic Cursor" (Weeks 1-3)
- **What ships:** parent-side and kid-side menu bar apps. Mom presses one button (menu bar icon, global hotkey, or **local-hotword listener — *no Claude in this path in Phase 1***, the existing Claude voice route is gated behind a Phase 1 feature flag and stays off on senior-side) → push notification to kid → kid taps to accept → Mom's screen is streamed to kid + bidirectional voice + kid's clicks on a streamed preview translate to coordinates and arrive over a WebRTC data channel as `POINT(x,y,screen)` messages. The existing Clicky cursor overlay on Mom's Mac flies to those coordinates with TTS narrating any kid-typed annotation ("Mom, click here").
- **Mechanism:** reuses existing `[POINT:x,y:label:screenN]` pipeline verbatim — only the message *source* changes from "Claude" to "kid-app over data channel." No CGEvent injection. No remote click. Mom does the actual clicking herself.
- **Async fallback (in scope, Phase 1):** if kid doesn't accept within 60 seconds, parent-side app records 30 seconds of screen + audio, queues for kid to review later. Not a separate product — just the timeout path of the same flow.
- **Redaction:** active-app and active-URL filter runs continuously; suspends the stream and shows kid-side "paused — banking detected" if it matches the blocked list.
- **No AI in Phase 1.** Claude is not in the cursor loop yet.

### Phase 2 — "AI suggests where to click" (Weeks 4-5, gated on Phase 1 retention)
- During an active call, Claude (with screenshot context, Worker-proxied as today) generates `POINT(x,y)` suggestions on the kid's preview as ghost-cursors. Kid taps to confirm → real cursor flies on Mom's screen. Kid still drives, AI accelerates.
- **Gate to ship Phase 2:** at least 2 of 4 Phase 1 households used it weekly without prompting for 2 weeks (Week 4 retention bar).

### Phase 3 — "Mom-confirmed AI actions" (Weeks 6-8, gated on Phase 2 working)
- Claude can now propose actual clicks/types/scrolls (via existing `CGEvent`-based one-click guided action mode in current Clicky, expanded to multi-step). **Mom confirms each action** with a single voice "yes" or panel button. Kid is no longer required in the loop for routine tasks.
- Hard scope: only the curated v1 task list (the 3 tasks from Sunday observation, hand-tuned).

### Phase 4+ — "Autonomous routine tasks" (Months 3+, only if Phase 3 trust holds)
- Skip the per-action confirmation for the most-used routine tasks. Mom presses a single "do this thing" button per task. Kid-side dashboard shows what ran, what got escalated.

## Approaches Considered (and rejected)

- **Solo agent on Mom's Mac, no kid in loop:** rejected. 6-month build, distribution problem unsolved (Mom doesn't install apps), the kid-tax pain (the actual demand) goes untouched until late.
- **Async cursor-narrated tutor (record/play-back model):** rejected as the primary product. Folded into Phase 1 as the kid-unavailable fallback. Async alone fails the mid-task-panic use case.
- **CGEvent injection from kid's machine to Mom's machine in Phase 1:** rejected on feasibility (multi-week sub-project, NAT/auth complexity) and trust (remote keyboard/mouse on parent's bank is a non-starter). Phase 3 surfaces this only with Mom-side confirmation.

## Open Questions

1. **Three v1 tasks** — resolved by Sunday observation.
2. **Networking layer:** WebRTC peer-to-peer vs server-relayed via Cloudflare Worker. P2P needs TURN servers (cost not yet budgeted). Worker-relayed media is bandwidth-expensive but operationally simpler. **Default for Phase 1: WebRTC with public STUN, accept that ~20% of senior home networks will fall back to a TURN relay (provision a small TURN server, ~$10/mo).**
3. **One-button trigger UX on Mom's side:** menu bar icon + global hotkey + voice phrase ("hey Clicky, help me") all wired in Phase 1 — let Sunday observation tell us which Mom finds first. No Stream Deck or hardware in Phase 1.
4. **Identity / pairing:** 6-digit code typed on Mom's Mac during in-person install. No accounts, no email, no password. Pair code locked to a kid's Apple-ID-derived public key (no Apple ID typed by Mom).
5. **Branding:** continue as "Clicky" through Phase 4. Re-evaluate at first-paying-customer milestone.

## Distribution Plan

- **Senior side:** No download, no signup. Kid installs over family-visit during in-person help session. App is invisible-by-default — one menu bar icon, one keyboard hotkey, one voice phrase.
- **Kid side:** Direct download (signed, notarized Mac app from website). Pairing flow: kid installs on their Mac → generates 6-digit pair code → kid types code into Mom's Mac during install. One-time setup.
- **TCC / permissions checklist (kid runs through this in person on Mom's Mac):**
  1. Screen Recording — required for the senior-side stream.
  2. Microphone — required for voice loop.
  3. Accessibility — required by Phase 3+ for click-on-confirmation. **In Phase 1 this is requested but optional; if denied, voice + visual-cursor still work.**
  4. Notifications — required for the "I need help" trigger to ring kid's phone.
- **Code signing & notarization (Phase 1):** two distinct app targets (senior-helper, kid-driver) in same Xcode project, both Apple-Developer-signed and notarized. **Sparkle silent-update + WCAG AA contrast audit deferred to Phase 2** to keep Phase 1 scope honest. Phase 1 senior-side updates are manual via TestFlight or kid-installs-new-build during the next visit.
- **Pricing:** Free for Phase 1-2 (founder + friends). $20/mo per **kid-buyer** starting Phase 3. One subscription covers up to two paired senior Macs (covers Mom and Dad in same household). Additional senior Macs $10/mo each.
- **No App Store distribution attempted before 100 paying customers.**

## Eng Review Decisions (2026-05-09 — these supersede the original Phase 1 description above)

The original Phase 1 description used "two app targets," "screen streamed to kid," "WebRTC stack," "blocklist redaction." After /plan-eng-review, the architecture is:

| # | Decision | Locked choice | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | App target architecture | **Single app, role-switch on first launch** | One bundle, one notarization, one Sparkle channel, one TCC grant per Mac. Senior-side UI hides kid views via runtime mode flag. |
| 2 | Signaling + TURN | **Cloudflare Worker + Durable Objects + Cloudflare TURN** | Extends existing Worker. DO-per-pair holds ~3s of signaling state. Same infra/deploy/auth as today's Clicky proxy. |
| 3 | Stream model | **Screenshot-on-demand, not continuous video** | "Stuck and frozen" use case doesn't need 30fps. ~95% less bandwidth, no video pipeline, simpler redaction window, smaller engineering surface. |
| 4 | Redaction posture | **Per-session consent + thin blocklist safety net** | Mom confirms per call, big visible status, one-tap stop. Thin blocklist (banks, health portals, password managers) catches the obvious. Senior-friendly version of "secure by default." |
| 5 | Code decomposition | **Decompose CompanionManager BEFORE Phase 1 work** | Extract `RoleManager`, `PairingManager`, `RemoteSessionManager` as new managers. CompanionManager consumes via composition. ~1 day of refactor. Tests tractable. |
| 6 | Audio pipeline ownership | **Explicit `AudioSessionOwner` state machine** | Single owner enum: `idle / pushToTalk / remoteHelpDuplex / claudeTTSPlayback`. Prevents "mic in use" bugs and feedback loops. |
| 7 | Wire protocol on data channel | **Codable structs + JSON over WebRTC data channel** | `CursorCommand`, `SnapRequest`, `ClickConfirmation` as Swift Codable. Type-safe, versionable. String-tag stays only for Claude→local-overlay. |
| 8 | Snap encoding | **HEIC + downscale to 1440p long-edge** | Hardware-accelerated, ~10-30ms encode, ~50-80% smaller than PNG. No quality loss for click targeting. |

### Minor code-quality findings (single right answer, no decision required)

- `OverlayWindow.swift` (881 lines) — kid-side preview lives in a sibling `KidSidePreviewWindow`, not bolted into the existing overlay.

### Surface specs (senior side)

**1. Voice trigger (primary; "hey Clicky help me"):**
- Always-on local hotword listener (no Claude in this path; gated behind feature flag, off if user disables in onboarding)
- On wake-word detect: **TTS confirms** "Reaching Jacob…" — calm, ~5-30s while signaling. Mom hears the system is working.
- If kid accepts → transitions to consent dialog. If 60s timeout → kid-not-available branch.
- Menu bar icon stays visible as fallback trigger; global hotkey for kid-side debug only.

**2. Consent dialog ("Jacob wants to help on your screen"):**
- Modal, vibrancy background, 480pt × 320pt centered.
- Hierarchy:
  - Line 1 — kid name: **40pt SF Pro Display Semibold**, color `DS.Senior.Colors.fg`. (Optional: 60pt avatar circle to the left if kid uploaded one. Otherwise text-only.)
  - Line 2 — "wants to help on your screen": 28pt SF Pro Display Regular, color `DS.Senior.Colors.fg`.
  - Two buttons stacked vertically (not side-by-side; senior eyes scan vertically more reliably):
    - **YES** — 60pt height, full-width, `DS.Senior.Colors.accent.primary` background, white text 28pt Semibold, ✓ glyph + "Yes, share my screen"
    - **NO** — 60pt height, full-width, `DS.Senior.Colors.bg` background with 1.5pt `DS.Senior.Colors.fg` border, fg text 28pt Regular, ✗ glyph + "No thanks"
  - Reassurance footer: 18pt regular, `DS.Senior.Colors.calm` color: "You can stop sharing anytime."
- **NO timer-driven dismissal** — Mom reads slowly, dialog stays until she answers. (Auto-cancel only after 5 minutes of inactivity, with TTS warning at 4:30.)

**3. Menu bar status indicator:**
- **Idle:** Clicky icon in template tint, no dot.
- **Active sharing:** Clicky icon + 8pt **green dot** overlay (NOT red — green is "okay, this is fine"; red signals "emergency" and would scare Mom).
- **Reconnecting:** green dot pulses 1Hz with TTS "still connected to Jacob…" every 30s
- **Disconnected:** gray dot, TTS "Jacob is gone now."
- Click during active session: dropdown with kid name (28pt) + giant **STOP SHARING** button (60pt height, `DS.Senior.Colors.accent.danger` + shape) + elapsed time (18pt hint).

**4. Pair-code entry (Mom's first-launch, after picking senior role):**
- Full-window, vibrancy, 720pt × 540pt
- Hierarchy:
  - Line 1 (28pt): "Enter the 6-digit code Jacob sent you"
  - Six **44pt monospace boxes**, 60pt × 60pt, 24pt gap, auto-advance on digit, backspace navigates back. Each box has visible bottom border (`DS.Senior.Colors.divider`), focus state thickens to `DS.Senior.Colors.calm`.
  - **CONNECT** button (60pt height, accent.primary, only enabled when 6 digits entered)
  - Cancel link below in 18pt regular gray
- After valid code → "All set! Jacob can help when you ask." (success card pattern, see #6).
- After invalid code: shake animation 200ms, error text 24pt regular `accent.danger`: "That code didn't match. Try again." Counter shows "5 tries left", then "4 tries left"... lockout for 1 minute after 5 wrong, with reassurance "Ask Jacob for a fresh code."

**5. Blocklist banner ("paused — financial app detected"):**
- Triggered when active app matches the thin blocklist (banking, password mgr, health portal).
- Top-screen banner across menu bar level, 60pt height, vibrancy, `accent.calm` text on bg.
- 24pt copy: "Sharing paused — Jacob can't see this app for safety."
- 18pt hint: "Switch to another app to resume sharing."
- NO close button — banner clears automatically when active app changes off blocklist.

**6. Post-session success card (after Jacob's session ends cleanly):**
- Brief, calm, auto-dismissing card, 480pt × 200pt centered.
- TTS narration: "[Task] is done. Jacob says you did great."
- Visual: kid name (28pt) + "good luck!" subtitle (18pt) + checkmark glyph (`accent.primary`)
- Auto-dismiss after **5 seconds**. No manual dismiss UI.
- Replenishes the goodwill reservoir.

**7. Kid-not-available state:**
- TTS: "Jacob isn't available right now. I can record what you're stuck on and show him later — want me to?" (calm, no rejection feeling)
- Same 480pt modal as consent: YES (records 30s snap+audio, queues for kid review) / NO (silent return to idle).
- Recording explicitly stays on Mom's Mac, ephemeral, auto-deletes after 7 days (matches eng review's data-retention spec).
